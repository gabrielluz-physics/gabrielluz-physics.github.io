1. Directly relevant to relativistic two-body dynamics and gravitational waves

1. Time-domain extreme-mass-ratio scattering waveform through 5PM and 7PN

Binary black hole scattering in the extreme-mass-ratio limit: time-domain waveform
Andrea Geralico — arXiv:2609.30118 — preprint

What happened. Geralico computes the gravitational waveform from nonspinning two-body scattering directly in the time domain, at first order in the mass ratio, through 5PM and 7PN. The calculation complements frequency-domain first-order-self-force treatments and provides extensive symbolic information.

Important qualification: this is not the highest-PM waveform overall. Geralico's earlier frequency-domain work reaches 8PM. The new element here is the high-order time-domain waveform and its 7PN depth.

Why it matters. It provides an independent bridge between the PM expansion, self-force theory and directly usable time-domain observables, and gives useful limiting data for future generic-mass-ratio radiative PM calculations.

Very high relevance Useful as an extreme-mass-ratio benchmark for PM radiation, radiation reaction and B2B-style comparisons.

arXiv:2609.30118 Earlier 8PM frequency-domain work

2. Soft theorems meet PN tails, memory and RG logarithms

Classical soft theorems meet the post-Newtonian expansion
Samim Akhtar, Riccardo Sturani — arXiv:2609.29895 — preprint

What happened. Akhtar and Sturani analyze emitted gravitational radiation propagating through the static background curvature of a compact binary in the multipolar PN framework. They connect the infrared logarithms associated with memory, tail-of-memory and higher tail insertions to the logarithmic classical soft theorems of Sahoo and Sen.

They also discuss UV logarithms associated with short-distance sensitivity of the multipole expansion and the corresponding renormalization-group running.

Why it matters. The paper organizes several hereditary PN structures in a language that may let amplitude/soft information predict logarithmic terms without rederiving every contribution from scratch.

Very high relevance Particularly close to EFT work on tails, radiation and renormalization.

arXiv:2609.29895

3. Black Hole Perturbation Toolkit gains high-PN analytic machinery

Black Hole Perturbation Toolkit: Low frequency and post-Newtonian expansions
Jakob Neef, Chris Kavanagh, Adrian Ottewill — arXiv:2609.25281 — preprint

What happened. New toolkit functionality generates low-frequency/PM expansions of MST coefficients, renormalized angular momentum, radial amplitudes and phase shifts; PN expansions of homogeneous radial functions; and, for circular Kerr orbits, inhomogeneous amplitudes and fluxes at the horizon and infinity.

Why it matters. Extreme-mass-ratio PN information is increasingly important as boundary data for EOB/PM reconstruction and for checking generic-mass-ratio calculations. Reproducible analytic software lowers the cost of pushing to very high orders.

High relevance A practical generator of test-mass/high-PN information for PM-derived potentials and gauge-invariant checks.

arXiv:2609.25281

4. Quadratic gravitational-wave scattering by Kerr: NR and second-order Teukolsky agree

Quadratic Gravitational-Wave Scattering by Kerr Black Holes
Lennox S. Keeble, Hengrui Zhu, Lawrence E. Kidder, Harald P. Pfeiffer, Mark A. Scheel — arXiv:2609.25522 — preprint

What happened. Nearly monochromatic (2,±2) gravitational waves are scattered off Kerr black holes, and numerical relativity is used to measure the quadratic outgoing (4,4) response at twice the parent frequency. A daughter-mode resonance tracks the fundamental (4,4,0) QNM, and the nonlinear coupling depends on the full scattering state rather than frequency alone.

A semi-analytic second-order Teukolsky calculation reproduces the NR response.

Why it matters. It advances second-order BH perturbation theory beyond quadratic-QNM studies toward a broader continuum scattering problem.

Medium-high relevance More peripheral to PN/PM dynamics, but important for nonlinear GW theory and strong-field response.

arXiv:2609.25522

5. A 5PM-informed spinning EOB framework is extended to radiation and evolution

Evolution dynamics of spinning binaries in effective-one-body theory to fifth Post-Minkowskian order
Jiliang Jing, Sheng Long, Weike Deng, Jieci Wang — arXiv:2609.27251 — published in Science China Physics, Mechanics & Astronomy 69, 110142 (2026)

What happened. This is best regarded as an extension/application rather than a new independent derivation of the conservative 5PM dynamics. The authors build on their previously constructed effective rotating metric through 5PM, derive the Hamiltonian and stress tensor, formulate a separable sourced Teukolsky-like equation for ψ4, and obtain fluxes, radiation reaction and waveforms.

Why it matters. The methodological interest is in combining high-PM conservative information, perturbations of an effective metric and dissipative EOB dynamics in one framework.

High relevance Particularly relevant for the question of how PM scattering information should feed EOB waveform models.

arXiv:2609.27251 Journal DOI

6. Environmental dynamics: EFT renormalization of dynamical friction and ultralight-scalar binaries

Renormalized Dynamical Friction in Black-Hole Spacetimes — Jordan Wilson-Gerow — arXiv:2609.23192
Dynamical Friction in an Ultralight Scalar Medium across Coherent and Stochastic Regimes — Soumodeep Mitra, Jordan Wilson-Gerow — arXiv:2609.28641

What happened. The first paper formulates environmental/self-force effects through a Schwinger–Keldysh EFT effective action and derives a UV divergence in the environmental-wake contribution together with the required renormalization and matching. The second studies binaries moving in ultralight-scalar media and identifies long-wavelength dissipation, a universal Coulomb logarithm and stochastic fluctuations of the orbit-averaged energy flux.

Why it matters. Environmental corrections are being recast as a controlled EFT problem involving renormalization, curved-spacetime Green functions and self-force regularization.

High methodological relevance

arXiv:2609.23192 arXiv:2609.28641

7. A compact analytic method for periastron advance generates new finite-size terms

Periastron advance from the perturbed Binet equation
Quentin Henry — arXiv:2609.29907 — preprint

What happened. Poincaré–Lindstedt perturbation theory is applied directly to the Binet equation, with secular terms determining the radial-frequency shift and hence periastron advance. The approach handles polynomial, logarithmic and inverse-power perturbations and multiple simultaneous expansion parameters.

The paper reproduces known compact-binary results and derives new terms from arbitrary mass/current tidal multipoles, spin-induced multipoles, NNLO current-quadrupole tides and several electromagnetic corrections.

Why it matters. It offers a compact route from conservative dynamics to a gauge-invariant observable.

High relevance Useful as an independent check on Hamiltonians, potentials and EOB information.

arXiv:2609.29907

2. EFT, amplitudes, QFT methods, and Feynman integrals

8. Hidden Region Finder addresses missed regions in Minkowskian Feynman-integral asymptotics

Hidden Region Finder: asymptotic expansions of Feynman integrals in Minkowski space
Einan Gardi — arXiv:2609.29442 — preprint, CERN-TH-2026-233

What happened. Standard geometric method-of-regions implementations work cleanly in Euclidean limits, where regions map to facets of a Newton polytope. Minkowskian limits can contain additional hidden regions. Gardi relates these systematically to pinch singularities and Landau conditions and proposes an algorithm to identify them.

The work tests this structure on massless four-, five- and six-point integrals in wide-angle on-shell, planar, collinear, double-collinear and Regge limits.

Why it matters. Missing an asymptotic region is more dangerous than merely having a difficult integral. A robust criterion for hidden contributions could substantially improve automated multiloop expansions.

Very high relevance Directly relevant to PM/classical-gravity integrals in Minkowskian and threshold/Regge-like kinematics.

arXiv:2609.29442

9. Six-point consistency strongly constrains the Veneziano amplitude

Six-point consistency and uniqueness of the Veneziano amplitude
Ilmo Sung — arXiv:2609.29919 — preprint

What happened. In a specified four-dimensional maximally supersymmetric planar setting, six-point consistency, SUSY Ward identities, meromorphicity, positivity and an unsubtracted forward dispersion relation constrain the four-point amplitude very strongly, forcing an infinite equally spaced tower of mass-squared poles under the assumptions used.

Why it matters. This is an amplitude-bootstrap result in which higher-point consistency is used to reconstruct highly rigid lower-point analytic structure.

Moderate relevance Structurally interesting for modern amplitude methods, though not directly a classical-gravity calculation.

arXiv:2609.29919

10. Light-ring ladder symmetry organizes QNMs, Regge poles and greybody factors

Accurate Black Hole Quasinormal Modes, Regge Poles, and Greybody Factors from Light Ring Ladder Symmetry
Rajes Ghosh, David Pereñiguez, Jaime Redondo-Yuste, Emanuele Berti — arXiv:2609.25228 — preprint

What happened. The authors identify a ladder symmetry in the high-frequency/light-ring expansion that maps the BH perturbation problem to an anharmonic-oscillator calculation. They obtain Schwarzschild QNMs, Regge poles and greybody factors analytically through 18th order in inverse angular momentum, with resummation.

Why it matters. It unifies several manifestations of black-hole response within one calculational framework and may generalize to rotation and modified gravity.

Moderate-high relevance Particularly interesting from the scattering/black-hole-response perspective.

arXiv:2609.25228

3. Other major developments in theoretical physics

This category was comparatively quiet under the intentionally high threshold used here. Rather than add routine papers for breadth, only two quantum-gravity/modified-gravity items were retained.

11. Can asymptotic safety generate a Horndeski sector?

Emergence of Horndeski gravity from asymptotic safety?
Astrid Eichhorn, Pedro G. S. Fernandes, Fabian Willaschek — arXiv:2609.29369 — preprint

What happened. The authors study two nonminimal scalar-gravity interactions generated in an asymptotic-safety setting. They lie in the Horndeski combination only for a particular coupling ratio, which their analysis can realize for a special cosmological-constant value. They also find indications of approximate radiative stability.

Why it matters. It attempts to connect a UV quantum-gravity scenario to a restricted infrared scalar-tensor operator structure.

Qualification. The result is a hint within the chosen truncation, not a proof that asymptotic safety uniquely generates Horndeski gravity.

Moderate relevance Unusually close to scalar-tensor gravity among quantum-gravity papers.

arXiv:2609.29369

12. Quantum gravity obstructs an exact extremal endpoint in a black-hole collapse construction

Collapse to extremality: The third law of black hole thermodynamics in quantum gravity
Gustavo J. Turiaci, Chih-Hung Wu — arXiv:2609.26875 — preprint

What happened. The paper constructs a classical process apparently driving a near-extremal charged black hole to extremality in finite time, then analyzes the same setup quantum mechanically using the near-horizon AdS2 description. The sharp extremal endpoint is replaced by an energy distribution whose probability vanishes at exact extremality and peaks at positive excitation energy.

Why it matters. It gives a concrete dynamical example in which the classical and quantum versions of the third law behave differently.

Indirect relevance Conceptually useful for black-hole thermodynamics and quantum gravity.

arXiv:2609.26875
